Direction A

The horizon is the grid

Stop treating the horizon as a logo garnish and make it the page's structural datum. Every section begins at a horizon line, and the sun travels along it as the reader moves down the page — masthead, first section, second section. The identity lives in the bones: strong horizontals, a left-anchored wordmark sitting on its own horizon, and deliberate "sky" — empty space above content that makes the name literal. Asymmetric, editorial, quietly confident.

Surprise
The sun's position moves along each section's horizon — a signature you notice on the second read, never animated.
Constraint
All identity expression must be horizontal. No badges, no panels doing identity work — only lines, suns, and space.
Density & pacing
Sparse sky above the lead; sections below run denser. The page breathes at the top and works at the bottom.

Direction B

The almanac of what's next

Identity through editorial craft: Plenty Ahead as a well-thumbed annual you trust, published daily. Lora's italic joins the system as the voice of the editor — datelines, chapter headings, sign-offs. Items are numbered in coral like an almanac's entries, every fact wears its "checked" date as a stamp, and each edition closes with a signed line. Warmth comes from print conventions used honestly, not nostalgia — the reader's sense that a person edited this.

Surprise
The verification stamp — "Checked Aug 9" as a visible badge on every claim. Trust turned into a design element.
Voice in UI
Lora italic carries an editorial narrator through the chrome: "In this edition…", "Worth your time…", "— The editors".
Density & pacing
Denser than the other directions by design — a well-set page of numbered entries, opened and closed by sparse italic moments.

Direction C

More sky

The most radical answer: identity through scale and subtraction. One idea per screen, monumental Lora, and the sunrise made literal — a huge sea-glass sun rising into the evergreen field, the only pictorial moment in the whole system. Coral disappears except for warnings. Every surface is either evergreen, sea glass, or open white, stacked like land, light, and sky. Nothing performs; the confidence is the identity. This is "one useful focal point" taken to its logical end.

Surprise
The giant rising sun — a brand element at architectural scale, built from two flat colors, no gradient, no photo.
Constraint
One idea per viewport. If a screen needs two messages, it becomes two screens.
Brand-uniqueness
A competitor's logo cannot sit on this page — the composition is the horizon, not a layout holding one.

How the three differ

All three honor the locked tokens, the 17px floor, and the calm register. They diverge on where identity lives.

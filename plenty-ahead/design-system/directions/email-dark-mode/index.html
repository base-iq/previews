Email Color Under Forced Dark Mode · Decided: Option A · August 13, 2026

Why this page exists. The first Email on Acid certification of the dailyv1 Daily (August 13, 2026) showed email clients force-inverting our palette: Open White #FFFDF7 turns muddy mustard in Gmail, Yahoo, and Outlook dark modes, and the Evergreen and Sea Glass fields render badly on iPhones. beehiiv strips all <style> tags at send, so there is no prefers-color-scheme escape hatch — the palette itself must degrade gracefully under hue-preserving inversion. Below: why it fails, and three token strategies that survive, each rendered in light mode and simulated inversion.

Method

How the simulation works Model

Gmail-class dark modes transform colors in HSL space: hue and saturation are preserved, lightness is flipped and compressed into a dark-theme range (we model L′ = 0.08 + 0.84·(1−L)). This is exactly why warm off-whites die: a near-white like Open White has tiny visible warmth but 100% HSL saturation — flip its lightness and that hidden saturation surfaces as dark mustard. A truly neutral white (saturation 0) flips to a clean neutral dark. Mid-tones barely move. Every "inverted" rendering below applies this transform to every color in the sample; a real Email on Acid re-certification is still the final proof.

Common ground

What all three options share Approved · August 13, 2026

Every option below is built on the same four email-only decisions — the options differ in how much surface they give the brand colors, not in the chemistry. No global palette value changes in any option: Open White stays the web canvas; the web palette is untouched.

Decide

Three ways to wear the palette in email Decided · Option A

Same chemistry, three levels of exposure. A is the smallest change from dailyv1 as built. B removes every filled surface — nothing left to invert badly. C leans into the sea-glass↔evergreen symmetry and gives the brand the most presence in both modes.

A

Neutral Canvas

Approved · August 13, 2026

The minimal correction. The canvas goes truly neutral; everything readers recognize — evergreen headlines, coral numerals, sea-glass panels — keeps its exact current values. Panels gain a mist hairline so their edge survives when tints flatten on low-quality displays. The evergreen footer field and sea-glass page surround are dropped in email (the two "field" failures on iPhones); the footer becomes typographic. Closest to dailyv1 as built, and nothing large left that inverts badly.

B

Structure in Strokes

Explored · not chosen

The bulletproof version. No filled surface anywhere — panels are drawn, not painted: a mist hairline box with a forward-green top rule. Ink on canvas is all there is, so there is nothing for any client to invert badly, on any device, ever. The cost is real: sea glass disappears from email entirely, and with it some of the palette's identity. The brand lives in type, coral numerals, and green rules.

C

Sea-Glass Banded

Explored · not chosen

The expressive version — it treats the inversion as a feature. Because sea glass inverts into deep evergreen, a sea-glass band is on-brand in both worlds: light mode opens on a pale sea-glass masthead with the evergreen wordmark; inverted, the same band becomes an evergreen field flying a mint wordmark. Bands bookend the email (masthead and sign-off), panels keep their fill, and the footer keeps its evergreen field — which inverts to the mirror image. The trade-off: this option carries the most filled surface, and large tinted fields are exactly what Email on Acid flagged on iPhones. Choose C only with a re-certification pass.

Recommendation

The decision

Option A — approved August 13, 2026, and promoted to design-system.md § Color → Email color. It fixes both certified failures (mustard canvas, misbehaving fields) with the smallest visible change to the Daily the team already approved, keeps sea glass — the palette's most distinctive surface — in the email, and every rendered pairing above holds AA or better in both worlds. B is the fallback if re-certification shows any filled surface misbehaving. C is the most branded outcome and worth a re-cert experiment once dailyv1 is stable. Whichever wins: NewsletterStrategist applies the tokens to the dailyv1 stylesheet from design-system.md, and the next Email on Acid pass is the real referee — this page simulates the transform; it doesn't replace the cert.

19 costs to cut when money gets tight

By the BaseLocal team Updated July 2026 · 9 min read

Prices climbed faster than paychecks again this year, and the hard part isn't wanting to trim. It's knowing which line items give real money back. We went through the budget the way we'd go through our own and found nineteen.

Nobody fixes their whole budget in an afternoon, so don't try. Read the list, pick the one or two cuts that fit your situation, and do those today. Most take minutes. A few are pure habit changes that cost nothing at all. They're ordered roughly by how much is at stake, with one exception near the middle that homeowners keep skipping.

Cut No. 1 · Credit card interest

Stop paying rent on your own debt

Carrying a balance at 24% is the most expensive line on this list. A balance transfer card with a 0% intro APR window changes what your payment does: instead of feeding the interest meter, the whole amount works on the debt itself.

The card we'd start with charges no annual fee, and its intro window on transfers runs well past a year. Move the balance, divide it by the months in the window, and set that as your autopay.

Cut No. 2 · Car insurance

Make insurers bid for you

Loyalty is priced in here, and not in your favor. The same driver, same car, same record can get quotes hundreds of dollars apart depending on the carrier — a spread you only see by comparing.

One short form pulls offers from several insurers at once. If your renewal crept up this year and you haven't shopped it, this is the fastest cut on the page: about two minutes, no phone calls.

Cut No. 3 · Debt over $30,000

Past a certain size, debt is a negotiation

When credit cards, medical bills, and personal loans add up past $30,000, budgeting advice stops helping. Debt relief programs put certified specialists between you and your creditors to settle unsecured balances for less than the full amount, consolidated into one monthly deposit.

There are no upfront settlement fees, and the initial consultation is free. Many enrollees finish in two to four years.

Cut No. 4 · The mortgage

Re-check the biggest number in the budget

A half-point matters at mortgage scale. If your rate sits well above today's, or you're paying card interest while equity sits idle in the house, comparing refinance and HELOC offers takes minutes and touches your largest monthly line.

Cut No. 5 · Timeshare fees

Close out the timeshare you stopped using

Maintenance fees average about $1,200 a year and drift upward whether you visit or not. Exit firms review contracts for consumer-protection violations, then negotiate with the resort directly. If yours qualifies, that recurring line disappears for good.

Cut No. 6 · Vet bills

Cap what an emergency vet visit can cost

An after-hours visit can run from a few hundred dollars into the thousands, and it never schedules itself politely. Pet insurance flips that math: a modest monthly premium, up to 90% of covered bills reimbursed, any licensed vet in the country.

  1. Answer a few questions about your pet.
  2. Compare the plan options that come back.
  3. Enroll online if the numbers make sense.

Pre-existing conditions are generally excluded — one more reason to price it while your pet is healthy.

Cut No. 7 · Bank fees

Fire the bank that charges you to hold your money

Monthly maintenance, overdrafts, out-of-network ATMs — those fees survive because switching feels tedious. It isn't anymore. A fee-free checking account that pays real interest opens in about ten minutes, and direct deposit moves with a single form.

Cut No. 8 · Online shopping

Let a browser extension do the haggling

Hunting coupon codes across seven tabs is a part-time job that pays badly. A free extension runs the codes at checkout automatically and flags when another retailer sells the same item for less. Install it once, then forget it exists until it interrupts you with a lower price.

Cut No. 9 · Subscriptions

Find the ones you forgot you're paying for

A streaming trial from March, an app from 2023, the second cloud-storage plan. A subscription tracker reads your statements, lists every recurring charge in one place, and helps cancel the dead weight. Readers tell us the first scan is usually good for two or three cancellations.

Cut No. 10 · Gutter cleaning

Retire the twice-a-year gutter bill

Paying a crew every spring and fall adds up over the life of a house. A micromesh gutter guard ends the cycle: the installer cleans and realigns everything once, then the cover keeps leaves and grit out. Estimates are free and start with a ZIP code.

Cut No. 11 · The roof

The one homeowners keep skipping

Nobody looks at their roof until water comes through it, and by then the invoice has a comma. A free inspection catches lifted shingles and flashing gaps while they're still a cheap fix. Fifteen minutes on someone else's ladder can head off a five-figure replacement.

Cut No. 12 · Home security

Protect the house without the contract markup

Security systems dropped the three-year contracts and the installation crews a while ago. A self-install setup with professional monitoring now prices below most cable bills, and many home insurers discount premiums for a monitored home — a cut that partially funds itself.

Cut No. 13 · Appliance repairs

One flat plan instead of surprise repair bills

When the water heater and the fridge both age out of warranty, every new noise sounds like $900. A home warranty rolls those surprises into a flat annual plan: a covered system breaks, you pay a set service fee, the plan handles the repair.

Cut No. 14 · Drafty windows

Stop heating the outdoors

Single-pane windows leak money in both seasons. Replacement isn't cheap, which is exactly why quotes matter: pricing for the same job varies widely between installers, and reputable ones estimate for free. Even replacing the two or three worst offenders moves the utility bill.

Cut No. 15 · Membership discounts

The membership that pays for itself in one dinner

If you're 50 or older, an AARP membership costs less than $20 a year and discounts restaurants, prescriptions, glasses, hotels, and phone plans. It earns its fee back the first week you remember to ask at the register.

See the member discounts →

Cut No. 16 · The coffee run

Three days out of five, brew it at home

We're not telling you to quit. A $6 latte five days a week runs about $1,500 a year, though, so make two of those days the good beans at home and bank the difference without feeling punished. No partner link here — this one's free.

Cut No. 17 · Lottery tickets

Scratch the scratch-offs

The average player spends several hundred dollars a year on tickets that return roughly sixty cents on the dollar. Keep the ritual, change the target: same corner store, but the twenty goes toward groceries instead.

Cut No. 18 · Premium gas

Your car probably doesn't want premium

Unless the owner's manual says required, premium fuel buys most engines nothing. At roughly sixty cents more per gallon on a weekly 15-gallon fill, that's close to $470 a year for octane your engine ignores.

Cut No. 19 · The phone plan

Same towers, half the bill

The big carriers' budget brands run on the identical networks for a fraction of the price. If your phone is paid off, switching keeps your number, takes an afternoon, and commonly halves the bill. This is the cut people put off longest and regret slowest.

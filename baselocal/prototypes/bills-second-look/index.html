12 bills worth a second look before the year ends

Bills have a talent for going invisible: you approved each one years ago, autopay took over, and the amounts drifted while nobody watched. Below are the twelve we re-check every year, ordered by how much money tends to be hiding in each.

1. The car insurance renewal you stopped reading

Premiums drift up at renewal even with a clean record. Carriers price loyalty differently than new business, and not in your favor.

One short form on Progressive's comparison tool pulls several quotes at once. Two minutes, no phone calls.

Compare your rate before renewal →

2. The interest hiding inside your card balance

Interest on a carried balance never arrives as its own bill. That's exactly how it stays invisible.

A 0% intro APR balance transfer card points your whole payment at the debt itself. Divide the balance by the intro months and set that number as autopay.

See balance transfer cards →

Intro terms depend on approval; a standard variable APR applies afterward.

3. The home policy you set at closing

Homeowners coverage gets bundled once and rides untouched for a decade. Pricing has moved since, sometimes in your favor.

A fresh quote from Lemonade takes a few minutes. It tells you whether your carrier is still earning the line.

Get a new home quote →

4. The phone plan (ask about 55+)

The major carriers run 55+ plans that cut two unlimited lines to roughly the price of one. Nobody moves you onto them automatically.

T-Mobile's 55+ plans are the ones our readers mention most. Same towers, same coverage, smaller bill.

5. The repair-budget guessing game

Once the furnace and fridge age out of warranty, every new noise sounds like $900.

A home warranty from American Home Shield turns surprises back into a bill: a covered system breaks, you pay a set service fee. Whether it pencils out depends on your appliances' ages, which is what the quote tells you.

Price a warranty plan →

6. The checking account that isn't free

Scan one statement and add up the maintenance, overdraft, and ATM lines. That number is the product.

A fee-free account that pays interest opens in about ten minutes. Direct deposit moves with one form.

Many accounts add a cash bonus for new direct deposits

Open a fee-free account →

7. The pet bill you haven't gotten yet

An emergency vet visit runs from a few hundred dollars into the thousands. It never schedules itself politely.

This is the one bill worth adding: Spot pet insurance reimburses up to 90% of covered bills at any licensed vet. The rate you get while your pet is healthy is the best you'll ever see.

Check your pet's rate →

8. The loyalty tax on your internet

Providers save their best pricing for new customers and count on you not calling. So call.

Ask for the retention department, name a competitor's promo, and be ready to walk. Ten minutes on hold routinely takes $20–$30 off the monthly rate.

9. Streaming, counted out loud

Most households land between four and seven services once the bundles are untangled. Say your number out loud — that's the intervention.

Keep the two you'd miss tonight and rotate the rest, one per season. Your queue will wait.

10. Prescriptions at the sticker price

Insurance copays aren't automatically the lowest price at the counter. For common generics, the cash or discount price sometimes beats them.

If you're 50 or older, AARP membership runs under $20 a year and folds in prescription, glasses, and hearing discounts. It pays for itself the first month you remember to use it.

Browse the member discounts →

11. The default electricity rate

Most utilities publish several rate plans and enroll you in none of them. Log in and run their comparison against your last twelve months.

Households that push laundry and dishwashing past evening peak often shave 10–15%. Checking costs nothing.

12. The gym you visit in January

Swiped the card fewer than three times last month? Pause it — most gyms have a freeze option they don't advertise.

Rejoin in the fall if you miss it. They'll take you back; they always do.

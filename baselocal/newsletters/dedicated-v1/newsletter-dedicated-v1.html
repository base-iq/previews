The most expensive things in your house are the least protected

A few days ago we wrote about the gap between what homeowners insurance promises and what it will pay for. Judging by the response, it hit a nerve. So today, the full picture.

Insurance is built for sudden disasters. A tree through the roof, a kitchen fire, a pipe that bursts and floods the basement. What it's not built for is age. When something wears out, the claim gets denied as “normal wear and tear” — and that phrase quietly excludes almost everything in your house that's likely to break.

What wearing out costs, in typical numbers

Replacing a central AC or furnace$5,000–$12,000
Water heater, installed$1,500–$3,500
Refrigerator compressor$600–$1,200
Electrical panel$1,500–$4,000

None of these give you warning, and most pick the worst possible moment. Heat waves and cold snaps are exactly when HVAC equipment quits, because that's when it works hardest.

That's where a home warranty comes in. One plan takes in your HVAC, water heater, plumbing, electrical, and major appliances. When something breaks, you request service, a local tech shows up, you pay one flat service fee, and the repair — or a full replacement if it can't be fixed — is handled. Wear and tear? No problem.

American Home Shield has been doing this longer than anyone; they effectively invented the home warranty in 1971. No home inspection required, no maintenance records, and no age limits on what qualifies. A 20-year-old furnace is protected on the same terms as a new one.

You pay a monthly plan cost plus a service fee per visit, so if everything in your house is newish and still under manufacturer warranty, you can wait. But once your HVAC or water heater is pushing eight, ten, twelve years old, the odds tilt fast. One covered breakdown can pay for years of the plan.
